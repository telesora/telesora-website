Nights are the hardest part.

In many care homes the doctor is in only a few hours a day. At night, at weekends and on holidays, a nurse or carers are on shift alone.

When a resident gets worse at 3 in the morning, staff often have one choice: call an ambulance. Some of these trips could be avoided with a doctor’s advice on the spot. For a person with dementia, a night in the emergency room means stress, a risk of infection and a step down.

What the AI does on every call

Four jobs, so the doctor and the staff can focus on the resident.

  1. Asks the right questions

    Guides staff step by step: what to measure, what to check, what to photograph.

  2. Prepares the case

    Before the call, the doctor sees illnesses, medicines and what changed today.

  3. Writes the notes

    Drafts the consultation note and orders for the doctor to check and confirm.

  4. Follows up

    Reminds staff, alerts the doctor if things get worse and, with consent, informs the family.

See the app, step by step

The doctor decides. AI does the rest.

The doctor

  • Sees the resident on video
  • Decides on treatment
  • Chooses: on site, watch or hospital
  • Checks and confirms the notes

The AI

  • Guides staff through questions written by doctors
  • Prepares a short case summary
  • Drafts the notes for the record
  • Sends reminders and messages

AI does not diagnose and does not choose treatment. Danger signs always mean: call 112 first.

Who it helps

  • Residents and families

  • Doctors

  • Health systems and insurers
